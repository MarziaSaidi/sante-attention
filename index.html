Product exploration

What happens when your POS starts doing the work?

Santé already connects the systems that run a store. I explored how merchants might supervise decisions as more of that work becomes agentic.

The question

As Santé's agents notice problems, prepare actions, and handle routine work, how does a store owner know:

what needs them, why it matters, and what Santé can safely handle?

Open the prototype → See the thinking

What I observed

The interface has a new responsibility.

Santé is bringing inventory, ordering, pricing, marketing, ecommerce, receiving, and reporting into one operating system.

As these systems become more automated, the interface must not just show information. It must help the merchant supervise decisions.

Today

Store data→Reports and workflows→Merchant finds what needs action

Hypothesis

Store data→Santé detects what matters→Santé prepares an action→Merchant decides when necessary

The product hypothesis

Attention

A decision layer across Santé.

Instead of asking a merchant to continuously check inventory, pricing, orders, receiving, and marketing, Attention surfaces the situations that require judgment and separates them from routine work Santé can handle.

Needs your decision

Consequential actions.

  • Pricing
  • Purchasing
  • Promotions
  • Inventory discrepancies

Handled by Santé

Routine, reversible work.

  • Synchronization
  • Categorization
  • Preparation
  • Background maintenance

Design principles

How I approached agentic UX

  1. 01

    Surface, don't make users search

    Bring important situations to the merchant instead of requiring them to find them in reports.

  2. 02

    Show the reasoning

    Recommendations expose the operational evidence behind them.

  3. 03

    Prepare before acting

    Santé can do the tedious setup while the merchant retains control over consequential decisions.

  4. 04

    Automation should be auditable

    Routine work can happen automatically, but merchants should still be able to see what changed.

Assumptions and research

What I'd validate next

This is a hypothesis built from Santé's public product direction, not internal customer research.

With merchants, I'd want to learn:

  1. 01

    Which decisions actually interrupt their day?

  2. 02

    Which actions would they trust Santé to handle automatically?

  3. 03

    Where do they need explanation before approving a recommendation?

  4. 04

    When does an “attention” layer reduce work, and when does it become another inbox?
