Product exploration

What happens when your POS starts doing the work?

Santé is bringing more of the systems that run a store into one operating system. I explored what the merchant experience could become as more of that work becomes agentic.

The question

When Santé can detect problems, prepare actions, and handle routine work, what should still require the merchant?

Open the prototype →

What I noticed

Santé is moving beyond software that simply records what happens in a store.

Inventory can inform ordering. Cost changes can inform pricing. Sales can inform promotions. Actions can move across POS, ecommerce, and marketing.

That creates an interesting interface problem:

As the system becomes more capable, how does the merchant stay informed without becoming the bottleneck?

From

Merchant finds the problem→interprets the data→prepares the action→executes it

To

Santé detects the situation→prepares the action→merchant decides when needed

The hypothesis

Attention

A place for the decisions that still need a human.

Attention brings consequential decisions from across the operating system into one place while keeping routine automation visible and auditable.

What I'd validate

I don't have access to Santé's internal product or customer research, so I treated this as a hypothesis rather than a redesign.

  • Which decisions actually interrupt a merchant's day?
  • Which actions would they trust Santé to handle without approval?
  • When do they need to understand the reasoning behind a recommendation?
  • At what point does Attention reduce work, and at what point does it become another inbox?
